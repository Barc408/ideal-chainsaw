## Page 3 — Day 1–30: Clarify the vision

### What to decide
- **Who is this home for, and for how long?** Forever home vs 10-year vs investment changes every decision.
- **Program by room:** a simple two-column list — "must have" and "would love." Most clients skip this and pay for it in change orders.
- **Lifestyle standard:** wake-up routines, hosting patterns, family rhythms. Your architect should ask; if they don't, you should tell.
- **Style references:** save 20 images, throw out 10. What's left is your actual style.

### What not to decide yet
- Finishes, fixtures, cabinetry brands, countertop materials. Too early — specs shift with architecture.

## Page 4 — Day 30–60: Assemble the team

### Roles you will hire
- **Architect** — design lead.
- **Builder (us)** — construction execution and often pre-construction partner.
- **Interior designer** — optional but recommended in luxury tier.
- **Landscape architect** — bring in by month three if the lot has topography.
- **Structural engineer** — your architect selects, but you pay.
- **Civil / geotech / soils** — required in most Bay Area jurisdictions for new builds.
- **Permit expediter** — in some cities, worth every dollar.

### How to choose
- Interview three of each key role. Ask for **recent-but-finished** projects (over 12 months old).
- Walk one finished project in person. The home two years post-handoff tells you everything the model home can't.
- Check for city fit. A great Atherton architect may not navigate Palo Alto's rules as fluidly.

### Red flags
- Anyone who tells you permits in Palo Alto take "about 6 months."
- Anyone who won't share a redacted line-item budget from a past project.
- Anyone who pressures you to sign before you have a feasibility budget.

## Page 5 — Day 60–90: Lock the money and the schedule

### Feasibility budget
By day 75, you should have a feasibility budget with:
- Land cost (if not already owned).
- Soft costs: 10–14% of total.
- Hard costs by CSI division: 55–65% of total.
- Finishes: 18–25%.
- Contingency: 8–12% (new build), 15–20% (remodel).
- Furnishings + AV (often outside the GC scope — decide where it lives).

### Timeline reality-check by city
| City | Typical permit window | Typical build window |
|---|---|---|
| Los Altos | 8–14 months | 14–22 months |
| Palo Alto | 10–16 months | 16–24 months |
| Los Gatos | 6–10 months | 12–20 months |
| Saratoga | 5–8 months | 12–18 months |
| Campbell | 4–7 months | 10–16 months |

Add 2–4 months for design if starting from scratch.

### Contract structure
The two contract forms we recommend clients understand:
- **Fixed price with allowances:** predictable, but rigid. Best for well-specced projects.
- **Cost-plus with GMP (Guaranteed Maximum Price):** flexible, but requires trust. Best for evolving specs.
Avoid pure cost-plus without a GMP on a luxury build. It's a blank check.

## Page 6 — Pre-construction decisions that save months

### Long-lead items to commit to by design phase
- Windows & doors (16–22 weeks)
- HVAC equipment (8–14 weeks, often more with VRF systems)
- Custom cabinetry (16–28 weeks)
- Stone slabs (variable — buy when available, store)
- Structural steel (8–16 weeks)
- Custom front entry doors (20–30 weeks)

If these aren't ordered by the time framing starts, your build stops.

### MEP coordination
Insist on a coordinated MEP drawing set **before** drywall rough-in. One day of coordination saves three weeks of rework.

### Allowances done right
Ask your builder to quote allowances at **the actual number for your finish tier**, not at a "standard" placeholder. A $50/sqft tile allowance on a luxury build is a budget booby trap.

## Page 7 — 10 questions to ask every builder you interview

1. How many projects have you completed in my city in the last 24 months?
2. Who, by name, will be my project manager?
3. What is your written change-order process and turnaround time?
4. What's your current backlog and when could you start pre-construction?
5. Can I see a line-item budget from a completed project (redacted for privacy)?
6. What's your sub-contractor retention rate over 5 years?
7. Will you put your allowances in writing at my actual finish tier, not a placeholder?
8. How do you handle owner changes mid-build?
9. What's your warranty and year-one check-in program?
10. Can I speak to three clients whose projects closed more than 12 months ago?
